### Brazil’s fiscal council Confaz will raise diesel ICMS by 11.1% from February 2027, according to Valor Economico.

According to Valor Economico, Brazil’s fiscal council Confaz approved raising diesel ICMS from R$ 1.17 to R$ 1.30 per litre from February 1, 2027; the 11.1% increase applies to the tax, not the final price. In Mexico, finance secretary Amador Zamora ruled out P-Caps support for state oil company Pemex in 2027, according to El Economista, which reported losses for a third consecutive year. For creditors and suppliers, replacement financing remains unresolved. Valor Economico also reported that the dollar closed the week at R$ 5.44 in Brazil, up 9.04% from the last week before the electoral period, while fiscal adjustment remains under debate. A weaker real raises the local-currency cost of dollar-denominated obligations and equipment for energy projects.

### Other developments

#### 1. Brazil: Confaz to raise diesel ICMS 11.1%, according to Valor Economico

According to Valor Economico, Brazil's Conselho Nacional de Política Fazendária (Confaz), which brings together state and Federal District finance or taxation secretaries, unanimously approved raising diesel ICMS from R$ 1.17 to R$ 1.30 per litre, an 11.1% increase, from February 1, 2027. The decision also adjusts fixed rates on gasoline and cooking gas. Confaz linked the update to instability in international petroleum-product prices.

Exposure: Energy, Logistics · Higher fixed diesel tax → Larger fiscal component in fuel costs → Potential pressure on operating and freight costs · Diesel tax per litre, Retail diesel prices, Road freight costs  
**Aldra assessment** (confidence: moderate, direction: deteriorating). The 11.1% increase applies to the tax per litre, not an equivalent rise in the final price. For operators and hauliers, the mechanism is a larger tax component in diesel costs; pump-price pass-through is not quantified.

**What to watch.** Implementation of the new fuel ICMS rates on February 1, 2027.

#### 2. Mexico: Treasury rules out more Pemex P-Caps, El Economista reports

According to El Economista, Amador Zamora, Mexico's finance secretary, ruled out using Pre-Capitalized Notes (P-Caps) again next year to support Pemex, Mexico's state oil company. The newspaper also reported that the company ended the first three quarters with losses for a third consecutive year and remained far from its Strategic Plan's hydrocarbon-production and crude-processing targets, despite expensive crude.

Exposure: Energy, Sovereign finance · P-Caps ruled out for next year → Future support instruments unresolved → Uncertainty over financing for a loss-making operator · Government financial support, Pemex financing availability, Pemex cash flow, Production and refining target shortfalls  
**Aldra assessment** (confidence: moderate, direction: unclear). Ruling out this instrument does not amount to withdrawing all state support. For Pemex's creditors and suppliers, the financing that would replace P-Caps remains unresolved while losses and shortfalls against operating targets persist.

**What to watch.** A Treasury announcement specifying financial-support instruments for Pemex next year.
